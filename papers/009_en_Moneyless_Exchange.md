# Paper 9: Moneyless Social Production Exchange — Feasibility Under Precise Demand-Production Matching

**Data principle: real data is sourced; projections are always marked "Simulated."**

---

## 1. Definition and Premises

Moneyless social production exchange is not a return to primitive barter, but **"direct value docking" under precise demand-production matching** — matching the production side's "surplus products / idle capacity" with the demand side's "clear needs," using "product/service value" instead of money as the medium.

It requires three premises:

1. **Precise matching**: big-data and ledger technologies clarify the demand side's "purpose" and the supply side's "capacity," without money transmitting price signals;
2. **Value quantification and equivalence**: a non-monetary value measure (labor time, product utility) avoids barter's "inequivalence" trap;
3. **Trust and coordination**: community organizations and credit systems resolve quality and dispute, without money as the "trust intermediary."

---

## 2. Theoretical Grounds

### 2.1 Marx's labor theory of value

Marx held that money is the "universal equivalent" — a *material carrier* of labor value, **but not value itself**. When production and demand match precisely, labor value expresses itself directly through a product's use-value, with no need for monetary pricing.

### 2.2 Time Banking: a real American anchor

Time Banking was founded by Edgar S. Cahn in 1980 and incorporated as TimeBanks USA in 1995, on the principle that **all labor is equal — one hour is one hour**. By recent counts (TimeBanks USA and published research): about **500** registered time banks and over **37,000** members in the U.S.; about 32,000 members in the U.K.; over **4 million hours** of service recorded across 30+ countries.

Cahn's framing strikes at the root of monetary monopoly:

> **"We refused to give money a monopoly on the definition of value."**

**Time Banking is four decades of lived proof that moneyless exchange is not a fantasy — it is a real movement with scale and cross-national spread.**

## 3. Three Viable Paths

### 3.1 Community "labor-time equivalence" (local services)

Members exchange skills for equal labor time, with a "complexity coefficient" for inequivalent trades.

[Simulated] A community pilot: 500 members, 280 exchanges over 3 months, 92% satisfaction, ~800 yuan/month saved per person. — Projected, not realized.

### 3.2 Regional "capacity-demand direct swap" (enterprise inputs)

Enterprises swap surplus products for needed inputs, with product value replacing monetary settlement.

[Simulated] A 30-enterprise alliance: 12 swaps in half a year, inventory down 32%, ~40 million yuan in procurement saved. — Projected.

### 3.3 National "shared-resource allocation" (public welfare)

Idle resources (surplus masks, medical equipment) are allocated to need, with "social value" as the basis.

[Simulated] A national platform: 20 million masks and 5,000 medical devices allocated in one year, idle-resource utilization from 30% to 85%. — Projected.

---

## 4. Supporting Conditions: Technology, Organization, Trust

Moneyless exchange is not "intermediary-free" but "replacing the money intermediary with technical and organizational intermediaries":

- **Technology**: precise matching + **tamper-proof value credentialing** (labor time, product quality);
- **Organization**: community coordinators, swap-review committees, third-party inspection;
- **Trust**: credit records + policy incentives.

**Tamper-proof value credentialing is the technical make-or-break for scaling** — without trustworthy exchange credentials, value disputes and forgery destroy the trust basis. This is the core function of distributed trustworthy credentialing.

---

## 5. Boundaries and Challenges

- **Fit**: labor-service and capacity-resource exchange; not monetary settlement, cross-border clearing, or complex finance;
- **Limits**: precise value equivalence, trust-building cycles, scale bottlenecks;
- **Technical make-or-break**: the absence of trustworthy credentials is the biggest current obstacle.

---

## 6. Conclusion

Moneyless exchange is the ultimate extension of precise demand-production matching. It replaces money with "labor time" and "product value," letting what the money system ignores — labor and time — re-enter exchange.

**It is a complement to money, not a replacement — but precisely this "complement" absorbs the part of five-fold idleness that money cannot.**
