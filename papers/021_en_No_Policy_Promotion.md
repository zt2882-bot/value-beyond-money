# Paper 21: Promotion Without Policy Support

**Data principle: real data is sourced; projections are always marked "Simulated."**

---

## 1. The Constraint and the Market Logic

The platform's promotion may face a long "no-policy-support" period — no subsidy, no license, no official endorsement. It must therefore **survive purely by the market**: win users by its own value, not policy dividends.

This constraint, in fact, constitutes a test of the platform's **real value** — *what the market chooses spontaneously is real demand; what lives on policy support will eventually fail.*

---

## 3. Key Risks and Responses

1. **Trust risk** (no official endorsement) → trustworthy credentialing (tamper-proof) replaces "official backing";
2. **Funding risk** (no subsidy) → technical service fees for self-sustaining;
3. **Compliance risk** (no license) → stay "non-profit, de-commercialized," avoid financial regulatory red lines;
4. **Capture risk** (success then re-captured) → open governance locks the original intent.

---

## 4. Conclusion: The Feasibility Boundary

The platform's feasibility boundary under no-policy-support depends on one core variable:

> **Whether it can provide tamper-proof, traceable exchange credentials** — because it must win trust by technology in the absence of official backing.

This returns to the report's technical make-or-break: **distributed trustworthy credentialing**. It is the only technical path for an open platform to "self-prove trust" without policy support.

## Full-Report Conclusion

This report began from **five-fold idleness** (capacity, land, resources, labor, time), passed through the layered diagnosis of monetary-liquidity mismatch, distributional imbalance, loss of purpose, and information opacity, and arrived at a set of **exchange mechanisms that do not take money as the only measure** — moneyless exchange, price-scale exchange, socialized e-commerce, producer alliances, and open matching platforms.

The landing of these mechanisms relies not on overturning money or on policy coercion, but on one thing: **providing tamper-proof, traceable exchange credentials** — so that "labor" and "time," for the first time, gain a value measure beyond money that can be trusted.

And that is the last thing the technical base of this report must complete.
