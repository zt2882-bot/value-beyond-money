# Paper 7: The Lack of Information Transparency in Monetary Exchange

**Data principle: real data is sourced; projections are always marked "Simulated."**

---

## 1. Money Ought to Carry Information

Money's ideal function is to be a **measure of value and a carrier of information** — prices should faithfully reflect supply and demand. In reality, monetary exchange is highly opaque:

- Where money comes from, where it goes, and the arbitrage and idle circulation in between cannot be traced;
- The "water" in prices (speculation, monopoly, information asymmetry) cannot be detected;
- "Money making money" in financial circulation coexists with "real production's" financing thirst.

**Money ought to illuminate supply and demand, but its opacity obscures them** — the technical root of liquidity mismatch (Paper 2) and matching failure (Paper 6).

---

## 2. Three Manifestations

1. **Distorted price signals**: asset bubbles push prices up, disguising surplus as prosperity — price decouples from real supply and demand;
2. **Distorted capital flows**: capital flows to collateralizable, arbitrageable fields (property, financial assets), not real-need fields (care, manufacturing, agriculture);
3. **Distorted need expression**: real needs (especially non-monetary "time" and "labor") cannot enter money's information system, which records only "how much you'll pay," not "what you need and why."

---

## 3. The Path: A "Money–Information Transparency" Linkage

The direction is to make exchange **carry and transparentize information about purpose, origin, and destination**. Concretely:

1. Traceable exchange information;
2. Tamper-proof value credentials;
3. Transparent supply-demand signals.

Landing these three requires a **tamper-proof, traceable exchange-credential technology** — the domain of distributed trustworthy credentialing, which turns "information transparency" from an ideal into an operation.

---

## 4. Verification

[Simulated] A capacity-exchange platform that records value, quality, and credit on an immutable ledger would see disputes over "passing off inferior goods" and "value disagreement" fall sharply. — A projected scenario demonstrating how trustworthy credentialing improves transparency.

Real-world reference: blockchain supply-chain finance and agricultural traceability both show how information transparency improves matching.

---

## 5. Conclusion

The opacity of monetary exchange is the technical root of five-fold idleness. Breaking it is not a matter of "stronger regulation" (which itself faces information asymmetry) but of building, outside money, a **credential system whose information is traceable and value verifiable** — a key bridge between money-information transparency and precise supply-demand matching.
