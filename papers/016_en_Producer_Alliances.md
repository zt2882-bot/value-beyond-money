# Paper 16: Producer Alliances

**Data principle: real data is sourced; projections are always marked "Simulated."**

---

## 1. The Concept

After disintermediation, individual producers' limited capacity (trust, cost, channel) makes long-term survival hard. The producer alliance arises:

> **A voluntary coalition of same-kind producers, taking on the trust, cost, and channel needs after disintermediation.**

It differs from traditional industry associations: an association *coordinates*; an alliance *cooperates* — joint bargaining, joint fulfillment, joint endorsement, with deeply bound interests.

---

## 2. The Necessity

After disintermediation, individual producers face three problems:

1. **Trust**: hard to build consumer trust alone;
2. **Cost**: high procurement and logistics costs alone;
3. **Channel**: no stable channel alone.

The alliance solves all three — **it is the organizational precondition for individual producers to survive disintermediation long-term.**

---

## 3. Core Functions

1. Joint procurement (lower costs);
2. Joint sales (shared channels);
3. Joint endorsement (mutual trust, shared guarantee);
4. Capacity mutual-aid (internal absorption of surplus — echoing Paper 9's capacity swap).

---

## 4. Cases and Experience

Real references: Western agricultural cooperatives (joint buying, selling, branding); Japan's JA (agriculture cooperatives); Spain's Mondragon (worker cooperatives with production-consumption-decision unity).

## 5. Conclusion

The producer alliance is the **"infrastructure" of disintermediation and the "coordination moat" of producers.** It lets "capacity idleness" be absorbed within the alliance — the meso-level solution for the "capacity" dimension of five-fold idleness.
