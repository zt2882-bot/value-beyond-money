# Paper 3: Wage-Price Divergence and the Crisis It Breeds

**Data principle: real data is sourced; projections are always marked "Simulated."**

---

## 1. What "Divergence" Means

"Wage–price divergence" is the imbalance between labor compensation growth and price inflation, in two forms:

1. **Stagnant wages, rising prices**: nominal wages lag inflation, real purchasing power falls;
2. **Differentiated wages**: growth concentrates in high-skill, high-monopoly sectors, leaving low-skill wages stagnant.

A shared fact across the three economies: **labor productivity rises steadily, yet labor's income share stays flat or falls** — wealth concentrates toward capital, consumption power is drained, and the five idle factors follow.

---

## 2. Three Classical Frameworks

- **Marx**: rising organic composition drives the profit rate down, while labor's relative position deteriorates — distributional imbalance is the root of overproduction and crisis.
- **Keynes**: marginal propensity to consume falls with income; concentration toward high-income, low-propensity groups shrinks effective demand.
- **Minsky**: stability breeds instability endogenously — leverage builds in calm, producing fragility.

All three point to one conclusion: **when distribution is imbalanced, the economy expresses it as "surplus" — and surplus is the five-fold idleness.**

---

## 3. The Four-Stage Transmission to Five-Fold Idleness

1. **Distribution**: wage-price divergence → falling real purchasing power → falling labor share;
2. **Consumption**: falling purchasing power → shrinking demand → unsold goods;
3. **Capacity & resources**: unsold goods → production cuts → capacity and resource idleness;
4. **Labor & time**: production cuts → layoffs → labor idleness → the unemployed's time idleness.

---

## 4. Three Economies, Three Forms

- **United States**: median real wages have grown slowly since the 1970s while productivity surged (BLS long-run data) — growth's dividend flowed upward, not downward.
- **China**: nominal wages rose, but housing/education/medical costs rose faster; growth concentrated in high-skill sectors.
- **EU**: the 2022 energy crisis pushed inflation above wage growth, shrinking real wages; southern youth low-wage-ization deepened.

---

## 5. Conclusion

> **Distributional imbalance is the first mover of five-fold idleness.** As long as growth's dividend fails to reach labor fairly, surplus and idleness will recur in every form.

The cure is not "stimulating demand" (treating symptoms) but **repairing distribution** — which requires a mechanism that restores value to "labor" and "time": moneyless exchange, labor as credential.
