# Paper 1: Idle Productive Capacity of Major Economies — A Five-Fold Assessment

**Data principle: real data is sourced; projections are always marked "Simulated."**

---

## 1. Introduction

This report examines the idle productive capacity and potential output of the world's three major economies — **China, the United States, and the European Union** — as parallel cases, quantifying idle factors across industry, land, resources, labor, and time.

The complete form of idle capacity is **five-fold**:

> **Capacity, land, resources, labor, and time.**

- **Capacity**: machines idle, production lines in surplus;
- **Land**: farmland abandoned, homestead land vacant;
- **Resources**: commercial real estate vacant, medical equipment idle, data dormant — assets *built but unused*;
- **Labor**: unemployment, underemployment, surplus labor;
- **Time**: everyone's leisure time consumed passively.

The first four are "visible idleness" with statistics and policy tools. The fifth — *time* — is "invisible idleness" with no statistics, yet it is the **largest, most universal, most ignored** of the five.

The direction for dissolving the five-fold idleness is the core of this report: **build an exchange mechanism that does not take money as the only measure**, letting all five factors re-enter the value cycle.

---

## 2. China: Five-Fold Idleness

- **Capacity**: industrial capacity utilization **74.1%** (National Bureau of Statistics, 2025 Q1), below the 80% threshold.
- **Land**: ~**264 million mu** of arable land idle (13% of total); ~71.85 million mu of homestead land vacant.
- **Resources**: commercial real estate vacancy ~15%; primary medical facilities' bed utilization below 60%; data as a resource largely dormant.
- **Labor**: ~103 million surplus agricultural labor; ~110 million urban hidden unemployment.
- **Time**: short-video users average 2+ hours daily — hundreds of millions of people's leisure time passively consumed.

---

## 3. United States: Five-Fold Idleness

- **Capacity**: manufacturing capacity utilization **75.4%** (Federal Reserve G.17, 2024), below the 78.2% historical average.
- **Land**: farmland idling and "Rust Belt" industrial land abandonment coexist.
- **Resources**: office vacancy **18.4%** (Yardi Matrix, Dec 2025), 27%+ in Seattle and Austin; ~20.5% nationwide (Cushman & Wakefield), with 108.6 million sq ft of sublease space. Remote work has left vast commercial real estate permanently idle.
- **Labor**: labor force participation **62.5%** (BLS, 2024), far below the 67.1% peak of 1997–2000.
- **Time**: the U.S. is the birthplace of Time Banking (Edgar S. Cahn, 1980), yet it remains marginal (~500 time banks, 37,000 members) — dwarfed by daily screen time.

---

## 4. European Union: Five-Fold Idleness

- **Capacity**: euro-area industrial capacity utilization persistently below historical average (Eurostat).
- **Land**: depopulating regions of Southern Europe (southern Italy, inland Spain) face land and housing vacancy.
- **Resources**: Southern European commercial real estate and tourism facilities cyclically idle.
- **Labor**: euro-area unemployment **6.3–6.4%**, EU **6.0%**, ~13.3 million unemployed; youth unemployment **14.8%** (euro area), **15.2%** (EU), Spain **25%** — nearly 3 million young people idle.
- **Time**: high-welfare populations hold large disposable time with no mechanism to convert it into productive value.

---

## 5. Comparative Structure

| Dimension | China | United States | EU |
|---|---|---|---|
| Capacity | 74.1% | 75.4% | below average |
| Land | 264M mu idle | Rust Belt abandonment | Southern depopulation |
| Resources | 15% vacancy, <60% beds | 18–20% office vacancy | cyclical tourism idle |
| Labor | 103M surplus | 62.5% participation | 15% youth unemployment |
| Time | 2h+ short-video daily | screen time, marginal time banks | high-welfare leisure mismatch |

**Observation 1**: all three economies sit at 74–75% capacity utilization — not coincidence, but the *same mechanism* (capital-driven overproduction) projected across different systems.

**Observation 2**: "time" is the only dimension of idleness that crosses all systems, cultures, and income levels — capacity idleness is Chinese, youth unemployment is European, but "scrolling short videos" is universal.

## 7. Conclusion

China, the United States, and the EU each idle **capacity, land, resources, labor, and time** in their own forms.

The first four have statistics and policy tools. Only *time* has neither — yet it is the **largest, most universal, most ignored** of the five.

Because dissolving time idleness relies on nothing but one thing: **turning "time spent scrolling" into "time exchanged for value."** And that is the most direct entrance to moneyless exchange — the core of this report's subsequent chapters.
